Top three points of stage divergence

Quarters ranked by the spread between the hottest and coldest stage composite scores — moments when the funding stack was moving out of lockstep.

  • Q1 2022 — Seed alone in Extreme Hot. Seed registered +3.11 (its highest reading ever) while Series A and Series B were merely Hot (+1.17 and +0.95) and Series C was Neutral (+0.49). Seed deployment was nearly two full standard deviations more extreme than the rest of the funding stack in the same quarter. This captures the well-known lag in Seed deployment during the 2021–2022 cycle: the proliferation of pre-seed and solo-GP funds kept flooding the earliest stage with capital well after later stages had already begun their correction. z-score range: 2.62
  • Q3 2021 — Series A overheats beyond an already-hot market. Series A hit +3.74, 1.15 standard deviations above the next-closest stage (Series B at +2.59) and more than 2 standard deviations above Seed (+1.34). This is the single highest regime reading for any stage in the entire dataset. Series A had the most headroom for valuation inflation in the 2021 boom: unlike Seed (constrained by round sizes) or Series C (constrained by viable late-stage company count), Series A saw both round sizes and valuations expand simultaneously. z-score range: 2.40
  • Q1 2012 — Seed hot while later stages were cold. Seed registered Hot at +1.27 while Series B was Cold at −1.12 and Series C was Cold at −0.69, with Series A near neutral (+0.38). This quarter is one of only two in the entire dataset where Seed moved in the opposite regime direction from the rest of the funding stack. It reflects the post-GFC micro-VC formation wave: Seed activity expanded rapidly 2–3 years ahead of institutional Series A/B/C deployment returning to growth. z-score range: 2.39

Sample construction

The sample is based on Cole Rotman's published list of $5B+ Series A outcomes, with several modifications:

  • Excluded: Any non-US companies on the original list (to maintain consistency with the US VC deployment dataset used as the independent variable); Waymo and xAI (whose deal dynamics don't align with typical Series A/B/C rounds); and io (which didn't have traditional venture funding rounds before being acquired by OpenAI).
  • Added: Solana and Crusoe. Both meet the $5B+ outcome threshold and followed conventional early-stage fundraising paths consistent with the rest of the sample.

Data source note

Funding-round data for all companies was sourced from Crunchbase. Where Crunchbase classifications are imperfect, we generally deferred to them anyway to maintain consistency with the US VC deployment dataset used as the independent variable (which is itself built from the same Crunchbase taxonomy). Exceptions: Solana and OpenAI were reconstructed from primary and secondary sources because the Crunchbase funding histories were materially inaccurate; and FalconX's Series A, Bilt's Series A, Kalshi's Series B, and Lyra Health's Series B were manually re-categorized due to individual round misclassifications in Crunchbase.
